05 The Read

Listing vs. what he told you: his public Facebook ad is $6,000, "clean title, nothing rotting out." He quoted you $3,500 in person. That ~$2,500 private drop off his own asking is the tell — he knows the frame is what you saw, and he's pricing it for a buyer who caught it. Don't let "nothing rotting out" move you off the rust. And note: clean title ≠ clean frame — the title can be legitimately clean while the subframe is rotted; they're different things. Use the gap: "your ad says clean, but we both looked at that frame — that's why you're at $3,500."

Seller's at $3,500. With a confirmed rusty frame, $3,000 is fair and defensible — the frame is your leverage. Open at $2,700 ("it needs frame work before it'll even pass inspection") and let him pull you to $3,000. Don't go above $3,200; past that the margin gets thin once welding's in.

The 4×4 is what flips this from a pass to a yes. A 4×4 Ranger sells far easier than a 2WD in this region, and the 132k miles is genuinely below average for a 15-year-old truck — both are real selling points, not spin.

The one thing that decides this deal: the $1,500 has to actually make the frame inspection-legal. Get a welder to confirm it on a lift before you buy, not after. If it's worse than the seller let on, the estimate blows up and the margin with it.

When you resell: disclose the repaired frame and price it "frame repaired, passes inspection." A hidden repair comes back on you; a disclosed one that passes inspection sells clean and still clears the spread. And count your time — ~$2k gross is a few weekends of work.

Not financial advice — this is deal math, not a recommendation. Verify the frame and inspection status in person before committing a dollar.
