Who to sell to, and how — the plan

The offer (custom AI software you own · $175/hr tokens-included · server installs from ~$1,200) wins against expensive, ill-fitting vertical SaaS. This is who's trapped on that SaaS, why they'll switch, and a 90-day plan to land the first handful.

Who this is for (ICP)

Owner-operated local businesses, ~$500k–$10M revenue, in the Hudson Valley / Westchester, who are paying $3,000–$15,000+/year for vertical SaaS (Mindbody, Jobber, ServiceTitan, Toast, Square, Shopify) that doesn't quite fit, marks up their card payments, and locks them in.

The decision-maker is the owner — not a procurement committee. They care about the software working and the bill going down, and they appreciate talking to the person who actually builds it. That's your whole edge: principal-led, owned software, one-time cost, on a stack that already runs a local business (2nd Nature).

Buying triggers to listen for: "our software just raised prices," "it doesn't do X," "we pay per-transaction," "we're locked in," "we still do this on a spreadsheet," "we want our data in-house."

The wedge (one message, tuned per segment)

Core line: "You're renting software that doesn't fit, paying a markup on every card swipe, and you can't leave. I build small businesses software they own — on their own infrastructure, one-time cost, no monthly lock-in. It's the same stack that runs a skatepark down the road."

Lead with the pain that's loudest for them (price hike, payment fees, missing feature, lock-in, data privacy), then the proof (skateOS / Smart Lawn), then the offer ladder.

Offer ladder (match the entry point to the lead)

⏱️ $175/hr — quick win / "fix this one thing" 📐 Project — replace the SaaS, scoped together 🖥️ Server install from ~$1,200 — own it all (privacy/cost folks)

Hourly lowers the barrier to a first "yes." Land a small win, earn trust, then propose the full build or the server.

Channels — local-first, lowest cost-of-acquisition

1. Warm network + referrals (highest conversion) — 2nd Nature's vendors, members' businesses, your contractors, friends-of-friends. Ask every happy contact for one intro.
2. Local business orgs — Chambers (Peekskill, Hudson Valley Gateway, Beacon), BNI chapters, Rotary, Main Street alliances.
3. Direct outreach to the trapped-on-SaaS list (templates below) — email/DM/walk-in.
4. Content as proof — the skateOS + Smart Lawn case studies, a short "why own your software" post, before/after of a build.
5. Local social — Instagram (you have the skate audience), Nextdoor, local FB business groups.

90-day plan

Days 1–30 · Set the table

  • Finalize the site (done) + add the 2 case studies front-and-center as your proof.
  • Build a target list of 30–50 local businesses on expensive SaaS (use the "find them" column above + Google Maps).
  • List 10 warm intros from the 2nd Nature network. Ask each for one referral.
  • Join/attend one Chamber or BNI event.

Days 31–60 · Start conversations

  • Send 5–10 outreach touches/week (warm first, then cold). Goal: 10 discovery calls.
  • Offer a free 15-min "where could AI help you" audit (your closer) — convert to an hourly quick-win.
  • Land 2–3 paying hourly engagements. Document them.

Days 61–90 · Convert + compound

  • Turn 1–2 hourly clients into a project or server install.
  • Publish the wins. Ask every client for a referral + a testimonial.
  • Pick the 1 segment converting best and double down (referrals compound within a vertical).
